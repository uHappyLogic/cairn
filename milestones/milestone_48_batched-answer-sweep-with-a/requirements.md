# Milestone 48: Batched answer sweep with a fold tool

## Goal

Make the inline answer sweep (`answer-all-open-questions-with-recommendation`) record a whole run in one fixed batched shell call, shown in full and run as shown: for each walked question in `walk` order it lifts the pick, folds the decision under its `## Decisions` subsection through a new shell-callable fold command for `requirements.md`, removes the block with its option, and commits one `Recommendation-answer:` commit with the lifted line as body, skipping a failed lift silently and stopping on the first failure after a fold with the tree resumable. The sweep's setup collapses to its fixed-call floor, reading `requirements.md` whole and a compact view of the question document (id, question text, lifted pick) instead of the whole XML, dropping the per-answer `locate`, and retiring the `=====` separator that fails in zsh. The fold command is used by the sweep only and is not the sole writer of `## Decisions`; the three single-question answer skills keep their Edit-based fold; the rewritten sweep is hands-on checked in Claude Code only, with the Antigravity and Codex trees rendered and reviewed.

## Relevant starting state

### The answer sweep as it runs today

`core/skills/answer-all-open-questions-with-recommendation/SKILL.md` runs inline with no agent and no wording slot, so it renders into all three host trees identical but for `{{PLUGIN_ROOT}}`. Its steps are: resolve `<MILESTONE_DIR>` once; one `walk` call as the whole gather and order; one whole read of `open_questions.xml` and `requirements.md` with the file-reading tool, for reasoning only, with no fixed command shown for that read; then, per Short Title in turn, follow `answer-with-recommendation-procedure.md` from its `lift` (a failing lift is the silent skip, a succeeding one's print is held as the commit body), commit the two files through the commit procedure under `Recommendation-answer: <Short Title>` before the next question, and stop the whole run on a tool failure after a fold with the tree left as it is; then one end-of-run `walk` as the completeness check and the report (`Recommendations recorded.` or the no-op line, beside the contradiction pairs and the completeness anomaly). Its tool-contract sentence carries the clause requiring every tool call, the followed procedures' per-question `lift`, `locate`, and `remove` included, to be written out in full and never through a variable, alias, or function of the runner's own. The contradiction advisory is formed at fold time, one question at a time, against the live `## Decisions`.

### The two answer procedures and today's fold

`core/shared/answer-with-recommendation-procedure.md` lifts (`lift <MILESTONE_DIR> "<SHORT TITLE>"`, whose one printed line is ANSWER and whose text before the first spaced em dash is RECORDED OPTION) and delegates to `core/shared/answer-procedure.md`, which owns locate, analyse, fold, remove, cascade. Its fold (step 3) is a prose instruction with no tool behind it: add a concise statement under `## Decisions` of `requirements.md`, in the relevant existing subsection or a new one, as clean prose with no citation marker, as a separate targeted edit per decision and never a rewrite of the file, written before the `remove`. Its step 1 `locate` prints the block verbatim for step 2's reasoning and step 4's judgment, step 4 passes RECORDED OPTION to `remove --option` verbatim, and step 5's cascade is a bare `remove` per mooted entry. Both procedures state that they are followed by the single-question skills and, once per question, by the sweep: `answer-open-question-with-recommendation` follows the lift procedure, while `answer-open-question` and `answer-open-question-with-alternative` follow `hand-answer-procedure.md`, which composes over the same recording core, so all three fold the same way.

### Writers of requirements.md and the shape of its Decisions section

`core/tools/define_milestone.py` creates `requirements.md` from a fixed template: the `# Milestone N: title` heading, the goal under `## Goal`, then the empty `## Relevant starting state`, `## Decisions`, and `## Out of Scope` headings, each followed by one blank line. After creation no tool writes the file: `specify-milestone-starting-state` fills one section, `modify-milestone-goal` replaces the body of `## Goal` and touches nothing else in the file, the answer core's fold adds to `## Decisions`, and `review-milestone-requirements` never records a decision. In this repository's recent milestones the section takes two shapes: one `### <Heading>` subsection per decision holding prose paragraphs (milestones 40–43, 45, 46), or a few topical `### ` subsections holding `- **Lead.** text` bullets (milestone 44: 2 subsections, 7 bullets; milestone 47: 3 subsections, 15 bullets). No test, fixture, or tool parses the file.

### The open-question tool's reads and writes the sweep uses

`core/tools/open_questions.py` (1,270 lines) offers `walk` (the ids of the recommendation-bearing blocks, one per line, dependencies first), `lift` (one line `<option> — <rationale>`, an `Error:` on a block carrying no `<recommendation>`; `--alternative` lifts an alternative instead; there is no option-only print), `list --with-question` (id, one tab, question text, in document order, composing with the two `--without-*` filters), `locate` (the block verbatim), `remove --option` (refusing an option naming none of the block's own alternatives, reconciling tagged dependents in the same write), and `sort` (annotated blocks first in `walk` order, so on a sorted document `walk` prints the annotated prefix of `list`). No single call prints id, question text, and pick together; a compact view today is `list --with-question` plus one `lift` per walked id. The output contract the tests pin: a read prints bare un-escaped values (a tab separates two values, which no value can contain since every write folds whitespace), a mutator prints nothing on success, every failure is one `Error: <reason>` line on stderr with exit 1 and the document byte-for-byte unchanged, and a malformed invocation gets argparse's exit 2.

### The batched-call precedents

`recommend-all-open-questions` step 5 is the one fixed shell call the goal names as its shape: per block an `if { err="$(… embed --recommendation …)"; } <<'EOF' … EOF then … lift … else printf … fi` section (the heredoc feeding the brace group, the form every shell parses alike), `body` grown by a literal-newline join, then the commit procedure's three steps written into the call with `git commit -q` and one fixed marker line per landed commit (`annotation commit landed`, `ordering commit landed`), from which the report step reads its status line; `err` and `body` are the one-quoted-value-per-call case that the shell-literal invariant in `CLAUDE.md` exempts. The alternatives pass's batch call (milestone 43) and the chained task-list calls of `derive-tasks`, `submit-task`, and both `complete-task` runners (milestone 46: the tool call `&&` the three commit steps, git output silenced, one marker line) are the other instances, and `complete-procedure.md` shows the move command alone while each runner's own file shows its complete call. The commit procedure itself (`core/shared/commit-procedure.md`) is guard, add, commit: `git status --porcelain -- <paths>` with every path its own literal argument, `git add <paths>`, `git commit -m "<SUBJECT>" -m "<BODY>"`.

### The commit record and its consumers

The sweep's commits are `Recommendation-answer: <Short Title>` with the lifted line as body, path-scoped to `open_questions.xml` and `requirements.md`; this repository holds 420 of them. `capture-milestone-principle-updates` walks that marker path-scoped to `open_questions.xml` and reconstructs each answered block from the commit's removed diff lines, so a commit must carry the block's removal, and revert-then-re-answer relies on one commit per answer. `docs/ways-of-using-cairn.md` runs the sweep headless in four chains as `claude -p "/cairn:answer-all-open-questions-with-recommendation" --dangerously-skip-permissions --model "fable" --effort high`, on another host only through the legend's swap.

### Field measurement of the sweep

The gitignored report `temp/answer-sweep-efficiency-options.md` (2026-10-18), reproducible with the scripts under `temp/answer-sweep-analysis/`, measures 88 runs since 2026-10-01 (856 answers across seven projects). A median run is 8 answers, 22 turns (setup 5, loop 16, tail 1), 1.9M input tokens at 96.7% cache share, and 2.55 minutes; the loop costs a median 2.22 turns per answer because the one-turn form needs the next question's `lift` and `locate` chained into the current commit call, which the skill never states, and 345 of 814 folds (42%) were `python3 - <<'EOF'` `str.replace` scripts because a file edit cannot be chained with `remove`. The whole-document snapshot's cache re-reads are 31% of all sweep input (the XML's share 15%), 60 of 88 runs needed two or more read turns, and the `list --with-question` plus one `lift` per walked block view is 9–13× smaller than the XML; 33 of 88 runs lost a turn to a read command separating two files with `echo =====`, which zsh parses as an `=cmd` expansion; the cascade fired once in 856 answers, and one `remove` and one `lift` failed. The report's option 3 simulation (the loop as one batched call) gives −77% turns, −86% input, −68% cost-equivalent, and −48% wall-clock, and its open question is whether the fold lives in a separate tool beside `open_questions.py` or as a `remove --decision` inside it.

## Decisions

### Contradiction detection

The sweep forms no contradiction advisory: its upfront reasoning makes no contradiction judgment, the batched call prints no contradiction line, and the report carries no contradiction pairs, so the sweep's fold-time duty to record the pick anyway and hold the pair is retired. In this milestone `review-milestone-requirements` gains a `## Decisions` consistency step that reads the recorded decisions against each other and surfaces a conflicting pair as a gap, for example as an open question settling which entry stands, flagging the conflict without resolving it. The next review pass after a sweep is where a contradiction is noticed.
